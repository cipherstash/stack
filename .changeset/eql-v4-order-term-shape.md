**An `eql_v4` order term is one hex string, and every `eql_v4` domain checks its shape when it is written.** A block ORE term (`ob`, and the `lo` and `hi` bounds of a block ORE range) is the lowercase hex of the whole ore-rs ciphertext, `"ob": "0202…"`, where it was a one-element array. Every `eql_v4` domain carrying `ob` must now hold a string starting `0202` or `0203`, and one carrying `op` a string starting `00` or `01`: an EQL v3 term or a term of the wrong scheme is refused with `check_violation` at the write or cast, not at the first comparison. The check reads only the prefix, so its cost does not grow with the term. In `eql-bindings`, a v4 `ob` field is Stack Encrypt's `OreBlockTerm` and an `op` field its `OpeTerm`, typed by the plaintext they order (`DateOrdOre::ob` is an `OreBlockTerm<Date>`), and reading a payload checks the term's structure. The `eql_v3` bundle is unchanged, byte for byte. A database that installed an earlier `eql_v4` bundle keeps its stored domains across an install, so it must drop its stored order domains first: see [U-003](../../docs/upgrading/v3.1.md#u-003-an-order-term-is-one-hex-string-checked-when-it-is-written).
